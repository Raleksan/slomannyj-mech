### Task-specific use

For **entity mentions**, encode a structured profile rather than the surface name alone:

```text
type=character
canonical_candidate=Жон Арк
mention=Жона
left_context=...
right_context=...
speaker=...
chapter=12
cooccurring_entities=Пирра Никос; Бикон
```

Dense retrieval should be unioned with character n-gram, transliteration, exact rare-token, co-occurrence, gender/number, and speaker-compatible candidates. Embeddings tend to over-link characters occupying similar narrative roles, so “Жон” and another student protagonist may appear semantically close despite being different people.

For **short event descriptions**, use an asymmetric instruction if supported:

```text
query: Найди событие, которое могло быть причиной или подготовкой этого события.
document: [actor/action/object/result/location/chapter]
```

Qwen3-Embedding and instruction-tuned Giga models are better suited to this than pure STS models. For same-entity retrieval, symmetric text-matching is more appropriate.

### ARM deployment

- `deepvk/USER2-base` is the safest ARM choice because it is only 149M parameters and uses an encoder architecture.
- `Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B` has community **ONNX INT8** exports around 0.6 GB; an April 2026 export uses ONNX opset 18 and dynamic per-channel INT8 and states that the graph is portable to ARM64, although its quantization configuration was designed around x86 VNNI. [huggingface](https://huggingface.co/majentik/Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B-ONNX-INT8/commit/dab2bed7de2cf8b278527f75afb6cf2a02fada80)
- The `qwen3-embed` package 1.4.0b1, published 11 March 2026, supplies ONNX INT8 and GGUF backends without requiring PyTorch. [pypi](https://pypi.org/project/qwen3-embed/1.4.0b1/)
- `jina-embeddings-v3` has official ONNX files, but the FP16 graph is about 1.15 GB and the full graph about 2.29 GB. [huggingface](https://huggingface.co/jinaai/jina-embeddings-v3/tree/main/onnx)
- ARM-specific dynamic quantization can be exported through Sentence Transformers/ONNX Runtime; community examples use `quantization_config="arm64"`. [huggingface](https://huggingface.co/dragonkue/BGE-m3-ko/discussions/7)
- Published Asahi/Apple-Silicon CPU throughput for these exact models is not available. Do not extrapolate x86 AVX-512 claims to Asahi.

For only 5,000 inputs, even a moderately slow model is tolerable as a batch job. What matters more is encoding each mention once, caching by content hash, batching similar lengths, limiting profiles to roughly 256–512 tokens, and testing INT8 cosine drift on your gold pairs.

**Recommendation for your constraints:** benchmark `deepvk/USER2-base`, `Qwen/Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B` ONNX-INT8, and Giga-Embeddings-480M on 300–500 Russian alias/event pairs; expect USER2-base to win operationally and Qwen/Giga to compete on recall.

### Can one replace LLM verification?

Not without task-specific training. These rerankers are trained primarily for **query-document relevance**, whereas:

- Coreference requires identity under contradictory attributes, chronology, type, gender, and mutually exclusive presence.
- Causality requires direction, counterfactual dependence or enabling conditions, temporal compatibility, and distinction between cause, motivation, continuation, setup, and thematic parallel.
- A high relevance score can mean “these passages discuss the same battle,” not “battle A caused battle B.”

A reranker can safely perform **high-recall candidate reduction**. A good architecture is:

1. Hybrid retrieval returns 30–50 candidates.
2. Cross-encoder reduces them to 5–12.
3. Hard constraints reject impossible pairs.
4. The 27B model classifies only the remaining ambiguous pairs as `same/different/uncertain` or a typed narrative link.
5. Accepted labels become training data for a small task-specific reranker.

There are no defensible universal precision/recall numbers for Russian literary coreference or narrative causality. Measure the candidate stage by **recall@K**, not precision: if gold alias recall@10 is below about 98–99%, the verifier cannot recover missing candidates. Choose the threshold from the PR curve of your own gold pairs rather than from MTEB.

A few thousand pairs are enough to attempt contrastive or binary fine-tuning of a 100–300M cross-encoder. Include hard negatives such as characters with the same title, relatives sharing a surname, two incarnations of a role, events in the same battle, and merely chronological predecessors.

**Recommendation for your constraints:** use `Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B` as the quality baseline and Jina v2-base as the cheap baseline, but retain the 27B verifier for uncertain pairs until an in-domain reranker reaches your chosen gold-set recall.

## 3. Names and morphology

No existing Russian morphology package reliably canonicalizes **transliterated English, invented fantasy, and Chinese-style names**. Morphology should generate hypotheses and features, not determine identity.

### Available tools

- **pymorphy3** always attempts an analysis using dictionary lookup, prefix stripping, and suffix prediction; unknown forms may therefore receive plausible-looking but wrong lemmas and grammatical tags. [deepwiki](https://deepwiki.com/no-plagiarism/pymorphy3/4.1-parsing-and-tagging-words)
- **Petrovich/pytrovich** inflects Russian first names, surnames, and patronymics and can estimate gender; it is rule-based, supports custom rule files, and is useful for ordinary names such as Иванов/Петрович. [ru.stackoverflow](https://ru.stackoverflow.com/tags/pytrovich/info)
- **Natasha** provides Russian NER, normalization, and a `NamesExtractor` that separates first name, surname, patronymic, and nickname using Yargy grammars and morphological features. [github](https://github.com/runotwo/natasha)
- **Stanza Russian NER** was trained on WikiNER and reports strong conventional Russian NER, but this is not evidence of reliability on invented fan-fiction names. [nlp.stanford](https://nlp.stanford.edu/pubs/qi2020stanza.pdf)
- Generic comparisons place DeepPavlov ahead of Stanza and spaCy on one Russian NER test, but all are domain-dependent and organization recognition was notably weaker. [mais-journal](https://www.mais-journal.ru/jour/article/download/1767/1362)

### Better normalization procedure

1. **Preserve the exact surface form.** Never replace evidence text with a generated nominative.
2. **Classify morphology confidence.** Record `known_dictionary`, `suffix_prediction`, `unknown`, and all plausible analyses.
3. **Learn paradigms from the novel.** If `Жон`, `Жона`, `Жону`, and `Жоном` occur in compatible contexts, treat them as an observed inflection set even if the analyzer mishandles them.
4. **Generate candidates conservatively.** Use lowercase, `ё→е`, punctuation removal, initials, hyphen variants, Cyrillic character n-grams, and optional reversible transliteration.
5. **Separate identity from canonical display name.** A cluster can contain `Жон`, `Арк`, `лидер JNPR`, and an epithet while displaying `Жон Арк`.
6. **Treat foreign names as potentially indeclinable.** For forms such as Ченьцинь and Сучжоу, do not infer gender or entity type from the final Cyrillic letter.
7. **Use narrative constraints.** Simultaneous presence, explicit apposition, kinship, speech ownership, group membership, location, and chapter-local co-occurrence are more reliable than guessed lemmas.
8. **Ask the LLM to choose among observed candidates.** Do not ask it to freely invent a canonical name; require evidence paragraph IDs and allow `uncertain`.

For Russian diminutives, use a curated graph rather than stemming: `Александр ↔ Саша ↔ Шура`, while keeping each edge probabilistic because the same diminutive can correspond to more than one formal name. For RWBY names, construct a small universe lexicon containing canonical Latin spelling, common Russian transliterations, team names, titles, and known relationships, but do not force fan-fiction originals into canon identities.

**Recommendation for your constraints:** retain pymorphy3 only as a feature generator, add Petrovich/Natasha for conventional Russian names, and make corpus-observed paradigms plus char n-grams, context, and LLM verification authoritative for invented names.

## 4. Quotation attribution

I found **no mature open Russian-prose equivalent of BookNLP/PDNC** with large-scale quotation-to-speaker annotations. Evidence here is genuinely thin.

Russian fiction does offer useful typography: a quantitative study of 400 nineteenth-century novels notes that new utterances usually begin on a new line with a dash. That supports high-recall quote segmentation, but not speaker identity. [sobch](https://www.sobch.uk/documents/manuscripts/2016_evolution_of_dialogues.pdf)

The closest Russian resource is **RusDraCor**, whose TEI markup assigns each dramatic speech a `<speaker>` and a unique character ID. It can provide weak supervision for speaker names and turn sequences, but drama has explicit speakers and therefore does not train the difficult attribution step present in prose. [publications.hse](https://publications.hse.ru/pubs/share/direct/220818959.pdf)

English evidence is nevertheless transferable at the architectural level:

- LitBank links quotations to the same character IDs used for coreference; it contains 1,765 annotated quotations from passages of 100 novels. [people.ischool.berkeley](https://people.ischool.berkeley.edu/~dbamman/pubs/pdf/Bamman_DH_Debates_CompHum.pdf)
- PDNC contains 22 complete novels and 35,978 quotations annotated for speaker, addressee, mentioned characters, referring expression, and quote type. [ojs.aaai](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/29870/31517)
- BookNLP scores a quote against nearby mention spans using contextual and positional features; its published speaker-attribution B³ scores are 86.4/89.9 depending on model size. [github](https://github.com/booknlp/booknlp)
- A 2023 sequential model using recent speaker and mention sequences matched more complex systems, which supports exploiting dialogue alternation explicitly. [aclanthology](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-short.64.pdf)
- A 2026 English joint-scoring system based on ModernBERT reports 94.5% overall PDNC accuracy, but this is not evidence that the model transfers to Russian. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.02359v1)

For your novel, implement deterministic sieves before the LLM:

- Explicit speech verb plus named subject: `— ... — сказал Жон`.
- Speech verb plus pronoun resolved to a local candidate.
- Vocative exclusion: a quoted name is more likely the addressee than speaker.
- Two-person alternation, but only while the active dialogue participant set is stable.
- Paragraph adjacency and interruption handling.
- Scene-local speaker roster.
- Consistency with first-person gender/past-tense morphology inside the quote.
- LLM attribution for unresolved cases, constrained to the local roster plus `unknown`.

Speaker assignments should become **soft coreference evidence**, not must-links. Incorrect alternation assumptions can otherwise merge two major characters across an entire dialogue.

**Recommendation for your constraints:** build a Russian rule-first quote segmenter and annotate 300–500 difficult quotes in Label Studio/INCEpTION; use speaker identity as a weighted clustering feature, not a hard entity merge.

## 5. Russian datasets and claimed papers

### Russian literary resources

Beyond RuCoCo, NEREL, and Razmecheno, there is no clearly established open dataset combining **novel-length Russian coreference, quotation attribution, events, temporal links, and evolving character relations**.

Resources such as AnCor/RuCor-style coreference corpora, RuREBus, Dialogue Evaluation datasets, and Russian news NER corpora cover useful subproblems but are not substitutes for literary annotation. NEREL itself contains 29 entity and 49 relation types plus events and cross-sentence relations, but consists mainly of roughly 900 Russian Wikinews articles rather than fiction. RusDraCor supplies explicit speaker and character identifiers for plays, which is useful for distant or weak supervision but structurally unlike prose. [hse](https://www.hse.ru/data/2026/03/30/230147842/nerel_paper_2024.pdf)

The practical conclusion is that you will need an in-domain gold set. Existing Russian resources can initialize NER and morphology, not validate book-level alias resolution or plot links.

## 6. Clustering and ranking algorithms

### 6a. Correlation clustering

Correlation clustering is the right conceptual objective: minimize positive edges cut across clusters plus negative edges retained inside clusters. It is NP-hard even in the unweighted case; randomized Pivot gives a 3-approximation for complete unweighted graphs, while direct weighted Pivot can perform poorly. [cs-people.bu](https://cs-people.bu.edu/ncordner/papers/byu_talk_oct_2022.pdf)

A complete ILP has one pair variable per node pair plus triangle/transitivity constraints. At 5,000 nodes that means roughly 12.5 million pair variables before constraints, so a global exact solve is not practical.

Use this decomposition:

1. Contract hard must-link connected components with union-find.
2. Reject the input if a cannot-link exists inside a contracted component.
3. Build a sparse candidate graph using exact forms, char n-grams, dense retrieval, co-occurrence, and titles.
4. Split the graph into ambiguity components.
5. Solve small components exactly with **OR-Tools CP-SAT**, `gurobipy`, or a MILP interface such as Pyomo/PuLP.
6. Solve large components with weighted Pivot or thresholded agglomeration followed by node-move, cluster-merge, and cluster-split local search.
7. Check all hard cannot-links after every move.
8. Preserve low-margin decisions as unresolved rather than forcing a partition.

Older Python correlation-clustering code includes Pivot, LP, ILP, and lazy-ILP implementations, but it is dated and CPLEX-oriented rather than a modern maintained package. COP-KMeans has a Python implementation with must/cannot links, but it assumes a known number of roughly spherical clusters and can fail because of greedy assignment order; that is a poor match for alias resolution. [github](https://github.com/Behrouz-Babaki/COP-Kmeans)

Exact solving is reasonable for ambiguity components of perhaps dozens to low hundreds of contracted nodes. The actual limit depends much more on graph density and triangle constraints than raw node count.

### 6b. Ranking 300 events

For \(n=300\):

- All pairs: \(300 \times 299 / 2 = 44{,}850\).
- Comparison sorting: \(O(n\log n)\), roughly 2,500 comparator calls in the ideal deterministic case.
- Sliding-window/top-\(K\): \(O(nK)\), useful when only the most salient events matter.

PRP formalizes all-pairs, sorting, and sliding-\(K\) variants with these respective complexities. Because LLM comparisons are noisy and non-transitive, ordinary quicksort/heapsort output should not be treated as a statistical ranking. [alphaxiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2306.17563)

A practical schedule is:

1. Compute your graph/structural prior.
2. Place events into coarse chapter-relative buckets.
3. Obtain 3–4 comparisons per event to connect the graph: about 900–1,200 judgments.
4. Fit regularized Bradley–Terry.
5. Actively compare pairs with close estimated scores or high posterior uncertainty.
6. Stop around 6–10 judgments per event—about 1,800–3,000 comparisons—unless bootstrap rank intervals remain wide near the turning-point cutoff.
7. Spend extra judgments only around top-10/top-30 boundaries.

That 6–10 range is an engineering starting point, not an established universal result. Active methods choose comparisons expected to maximize information gain under the current Bradley–Terry posterior. [ar5iv.labs.arxiv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2311.06093)

`choix` supports Bradley–Terry pairwise data, Plackett–Luce rankings, I-LSR, MM, optimization, and Bayesian inference. Its standard pairwise API represents strict winner/loser outcomes and is not an ideal native tie model. For genuine ties, use a Davidson or Rao–Kupper extension, or record a graded preference probability in a custom logistic likelihood. [choix.lum](https://choix.lum.li/en/stable/data.html)

Position bias is well documented and varies by model and task. Reverse every high-impact comparison; if `(A,B)` and `(B,A)` disagree, label it `tie/unstable` rather than choosing arbitrarily. Blind chapter number and current graph centrality from the LLM where possible so that the judge evaluates narrative consequence rather than late-book position. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.17788v1)

**Recommendation for your constraints:** contract must-links, exact-solve only small ambiguity components, use sparse weighted Pivot plus local search elsewhere, and budget roughly 2,000–3,000 salience judgments with order reversal concentrated near the top-salience boundary.

## 7. Extractor reliability

### 7a. Abliterated checkpoints

There is no strong peer-reviewed evidence specifically comparing **Huihui-Qwen3.8-27B-abliterated** with the original checkpoint on Russian extraction, JSON-schema compliance, evidence localization, or coreference. Any claim that abliteration is harmless for your task would therefore be unjustified.

Broader 2026 evidence is mixed:

- One controlled code-model study found that abliteration removed refusals while retaining more than 93% syntactic validity, but it only studied Qwen2.5-Coder 3B–14B on SQL-injection generation. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05396)
- A multilingual legal study found MMLU, HellaSwag, and IFEval changes within roughly one point but a 7.1-point TruthfulQA decline after abliteration. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.23375v1)
- A larger comparison found capability preservation strongly dependent on the abliteration method and model, with some configurations suffering substantial task regressions. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.13655v2)

Thus, abliteration may leave broad benchmark scores intact while changing truthfulness, confidence, or task-specific instruction behavior. Your model’s unusually high throughput does not establish extraction fidelity.

Run an A/B test against the corresponding unmodified Qwen checkpoint on at least:

- 200 entity mentions.
- 100 alias pairs.
- 100 location links.
- 100 event records.
- 100 evidence-span selections.
- 100 cause/setup pairs.
- JSON validity, field omission, unsupported-field rate, citation precision/recall, and calibration.

### 7b. Sampling

Qwen’s official Qwen3 guidance recommends:

- Thinking mode: temperature 0.6, top-p 0.95, top-k 20.
- Non-thinking mode: temperature 0.7, top-p 0.8, top-k 20.
- Avoid greedy decoding in thinking mode because it can cause degradation and repetition. [qwen.readthedocs](https://qwen.readthedocs.io/en/latest/deployment/vllm.html)

Those are general-generation defaults, not optimal extraction settings. For your application:

| Operation | Suggested mode |
|---|---|
| Local entity/event extraction | Non-thinking; temperature 0.1–0.3; top-p 0.8; top-k 20; fixed seed |
| Binary same/different checks | Non-thinking at 0.1–0.2, with swapped candidate order |
| Difficult causality/payoff verification | Thinking at official 0.6/0.95/20, but require a short structured verdict |
| Salience comparison | 0.2–0.4, both item orders, explicit tie allowed |
| Final summaries | 0.4–0.7 after evidence retrieval |

Use vLLM schema-constrained decoding for syntax, but remember that it guarantees schema compliance, not factual correctness. Qwen’s documentation explicitly recommends both structured decoding and an in-prompt format instruction. [qwen.readthedocs](https://qwen.readthedocs.io/en/v3.0/deployment/vllm.html)

### 7c. Paragraph citations

Open models’ citation quality is materially weaker than answer fluency. LongCite reports that ordinary open models lag proprietary models in fine-grained citation quality, while citation-tuned 8B/9B models improve substantially. More broadly, citation faithfulness degrades faster than answer correctness as context grows. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13276v2)

Do not ask the model to recall arbitrary paragraph numbers from a 16K prompt. Instead:

1. Assign immutable IDs such as `c012_p0042`.
2. Retrieve 5–20 candidate paragraphs before verification.
3. Constrain `evidence_ids` to an enum containing only those IDs.
4. Require separate evidence for each claim or edge.
5. Verify that quoted offsets are exact substrings.
6. Run a second entailment/contradiction check against only the cited paragraphs.
7. Store `unsupported`, `partial`, and `contradicted`, not just a confidence score.

FABLES decomposes book summaries into claims and stores textual evidence for each judgment; it also found that automatic raters struggle particularly with hard event and character-state errors. This supports evaluating evidence localization separately from extraction correctness. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.01261.pdf)

**Recommendation for your constraints:** keep the abliterated model only if it beats the original on an in-domain A/B gold set; use low-temperature non-thinking extraction, official-temperature thinking only for hard checks, and retrieve then select paragraph IDs rather than asking for free-form citations.

## 8. One-annotator gold tooling

No single annotation tool is ideal for spans, coreference chains, typed event-event links, and convenient JSON export.

| Tool | Strength | Weakness for your task |
|---|---|---|
| **INCEpTION** | Best overall schema: spans, attributes, relations, chains, document metadata, adjudication and recommendation support | Heavier Java setup; native formats are annotation-oriented rather than your final KG JSON |
| **Label Studio** | Fast Docker setup, configurable span and relation UI, straightforward JSON project export | Coreference chains must usually be represented as pairwise `COREF` relations or custom UI logic |
| **brat** | Very fast, lightweight, excellent span/relation visualization; NEREL used brat in separate entity/relation/link passes. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.11159v1.pdf) | Standoff `.ann` export needs conversion; long documents and chain management are awkward |
| **doccano** | Simple local setup and JSONL export; good for NER and basic relations | Less suitable for dense cross-document or book-level coreference |
| **CorefAnnotator** | Purpose-built chain annotation | Poor fit for event links and multiple evidence layers |
| **Argilla** | Good workflow and model-assisted review | Not primarily a graph/coreference annotation interface |

For one annotator, do not load an entire 1.35M-token book into one browser document. Create annotation packets containing:

- A focal scene or chapter excerpt.
- Stable global paragraph IDs.
- The local entity roster.
- Retrieved earlier/later evidence snippets.
- Candidate links produced by the pipeline.
- An option to add a missed candidate.
- `accept`, `reject`, `uncertain`, and `not enough context`.

A minimal gold set should include:

- 300–500 entity mentions across common and difficult characters.
- 150–250 alias candidate pairs, deliberately balanced with hard negatives.
- 150 location mentions.
- 150 event records with evidence and argument correctness.
- 100–150 causal/setup/payoff candidates.
- 300 event-salience comparisons rather than absolute importance labels.
- 200 quotations, oversampling implicit and three-plus-speaker dialogue.
- 50 summary claims with supporting or contradicting spans.

Because there is only one annotator, re-annotate 10–15% after two weeks and report intra-annotator agreement. For coreference, report pairwise agreement plus B³/CEAF/LEA against the first pass; for links, report per-type Cohen’s kappa or simple agreement with an uncertainty category.

I could not responsibly verify exact October 2026 release numbers for every annotation package, so pin the Docker image or Git commit you test rather than assuming “latest” will preserve export compatibility.

**Recommendation for your constraints:** start with Label Studio for the fastest local JSON workflow, encode coreference as typed pair relations, and move to INCEpTION only if chain navigation and annotation layers become the bottleneck.

## 9. Full-novel KG precedents

Evidence for an **open-source, end-to-end, non-English, whole-novel KG pipeline using a local LLM** remains thin. I found no 2024–2026 system that simultaneously solves your complete target: Russian NER/coreference, event extraction, global salience, fabula ordering, setup–payoff links, evolving relations, hierarchical storylines, evidence spans, and book-level evaluation.

The closest relevant projects address individual layers:

### BOOKCOREF

BOOKCOREF provides full-book English coreference data, code, and evaluation. Its paper reports that book-scale training/evaluation can improve long-document systems by up to 20 CoNLL-F1 points, while current systems still perform markedly worse on full books than on 1,500-token windows. [aclanthology](https://aclanthology.org/people/pere-lluis-huguet-cabot/)

**Lesson:** book-level coreference must be evaluated at book scale; strong local scores can coexist with severe global fragmentation and merging.

### EvolvTrip

EvolvTrip represents beliefs, desires, intentions, and traits as perspective-aware temporal graph states. It reports particularly strong benefits for smaller models working over long narratives. [arxiv](http://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13641)

**Lesson:** character properties and relationships should be append-only state transitions with provenance, not static character-profile fields.

### CFPG

CFPG explicitly represents **Foreshadow–Trigger–Payoff** triples and treats an unresolved setup as a narrative commitment or “causal debt.” Its dataset contains 629 validated pairs across 148 books. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.07033v1)

**Lesson:** retrieve both forward from setup candidates and backward from payoff candidates, and model the intermediate trigger rather than asking only whether setup directly caused payoff.

### LitVISTA

LitVISTA represents narrative events as a topology rather than a flat sequence. Even with gold event anchors, frontier models struggle with global functions and dependencies; end-to-end errors are dominated by anchor identification and localization. [aclanthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1024.pdf)

**Lesson:** separate event-anchor quality from edge/topology quality, and evaluate both. Better clustering cannot compensate for missing or badly localized events.

### NarraBench

NarraBench’s 2025 survey finds that only about 27% of its narrative-task taxonomy is adequately represented by existing benchmarks, with causality, perspective, revelation, and events among the major gaps. [txtlab](https://txtlab.org/2025/10/announcing-narrabench-a-new-framework-for-testing-narrative-intelligence-in-language-models/)

**Lesson:** absence of a standard benchmark is real; your small, precisely scoped gold set is more useful than claiming coverage through generic MTEB or summarization metrics.

### Book-length faithfulness work

BooookScore compared hierarchical merging with incremental summary updating and found a trade-off: hierarchical merging was generally more coherent, while incremental updating preserved more detail. FABLES found that the hardest factual errors concern events and character states and often require indirect narrative reasoning to refute. [semanticscholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/FABLES:-Evaluating-faithfulness-and-content-in-Kim-Chang/ba4548c01a8b29a406f269db80f7105b88ca9751)

**Lesson:** retain detailed evidence-backed event/state records incrementally, but construct final summaries hierarchically from verified records rather than repeatedly rewriting one global summary.

### Practical architecture

For your scale, the defensible architecture is:

1. Paragraph-addressed source store.
2. Independent high-recall mention/event extraction.
3. Running roster used as retrieved context, not blindly trusted state.
4. Reversible candidate entity graph.
5. Constraint propagation and correlation clustering.
6. Retrospective global salience ranking.
7. Bidirectional retrieval for long-range event links.
8. Typed LLM verification with evidence IDs.
9. Separate discourse-time and story-time graphs.
10. Event-sourced character/relationship states.
11. Overlapping hierarchical storyline clustering.
12. Claim-level grounded summaries.
13. Small but deliberately difficult gold evaluation.

The critical design principle is **delay irreversible decisions**. Stateful memory improves recall, but an incorrect early alias, chronology, or storyline assignment can contaminate every later chapter; retain hypotheses, confidence, and provenance until retrospective passes.

**Recommendation for your constraints:** treat BOOKCOREF, EvolvTrip, CFPG, LitVISTA, and FABLES as component blueprints—not an existing solution—and build a reversible multi-pass graph whose every entity, event, temporal edge, and narrative link points back to paragraph evidence.
